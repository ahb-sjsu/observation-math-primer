\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{exr:ch04:which}}
(a) The second derivative is $e^x+2>0$, so the function is convex. (b) The second derivative of $-\sqrt x$ is $\frac14x^{-3/2}>0$ on $x>0$, so it is convex. (c) The second derivative $6x$ is negative for $x<0$, so $x^3$ is not convex on $\R$.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{exr:ch04:slack}}
The unconstrained minimum $(2,1)$ has $x+y=3\le4$ and $x=2\ge0$, so it is feasible. Both constraints are slack, both multipliers are zero, and stationarity is $\nabla f_0=0$. Complementary slackness is what lets the KKT conditions return the unconstrained answer when no constraint binds.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{exr:ch04:halfspace}}
The Lagrangian $\norm x^2+\mu(1-a\T x)$ gives $2x=\mu a$. The constraint must bind, since $x=0$ is infeasible, so $a\T x=1$ and $x=a/\norm a^2$ with $\mu=2/\norm a^2$. For $a=(1,2)$, $x=(0.2,0.4)$, $\mu=0.4$, and the minimum is $\norm x^2=0.2$.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{exr:ch04:cwf}}
With both channels in use the level would be $(1+1+3)/2=2.5<3$, which is impossible, so only the first is used. Then $\nu=2$, $P=(1,0)$, and since $N_2=3\ge\nu$ the KKT conditions hold. The capacity is $\frac12\log_2 2=0.5$ bit per use.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{exr:ch04:eqdual}}
Minimizing $x^2+2y^2+\lambda(3-x-y)$ over $(x,y)$ gives $x=\lambda/2$ and $y=\lambda/4$, so $g(\lambda)=3\lambda-\frac38\lambda^2$. Its derivative vanishes at $\lambda=4$, where $g(4)=12-6=6=p^\star$. The dual maximizer is the Lagrange multiplier of \cref{ex:ch04:lagrange}.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{exr:ch04:mmfix}}
Up to a constant the surrogate is $-\ln x+\frac{x}{1+x_t}+\frac x4$. Its derivative $-\frac1x+\frac1{1+x_t}+\frac14$ vanishes at $x=\frac{4(1+x_t)}{5+x_t}$. A fixed point satisfies $x(5+x)=4(1+x)$, that is, $x^2+x-4=0$, and the same equation is $f'(x)=-\frac1x+\frac1{1+x}+\frac14=0$ multiplied by $4x(1+x)$. The update map has derivative $\frac{16}{(5+x)^2}$, which is $0.3717$ at $x^\star=1.5616$, so near the solution the error shrinks by that factor at every step.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{exr:ch04:bastep}}
With $q_0=(0.75,0.25)$, the relative entropies are $\ln\frac43=0.2877$ and $0.1438$ nats. The weights are $\frac12e^{0.2877}$ and $\frac12e^{0.1438}$, that is, $\frac12\cdot\frac43$ and $\frac12\cdot\sqrt{4/3}$, and normalizing gives $p_1=(0.5359,0.4641)$.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{exr:ch04:cvx}}
The solver returns $\Sd^\star=\diag(0.25,1)$ and $\frac12\log_2(\det\Sigma_x/\det\Sd^\star)=1.7926$ bits, the water-filling value of \cref{ch03}. With $\PC=I$ it returns the identity-reader rate $R(0.5)=2.792$ bits. The optimal $\Sd^\star$ for $\PC=\diag(1,0.25)$ equals $\theta\PC^{-1}$ with $\theta=0.25$, because both modes are above water.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{exr:ch04:bard}}
At $\beta=\ln9$ the conditional reproduction law is $p(\hat x\ne x\mid x)=1/(1+9)=0.1$, so $D=0.1$, and the rate is $1-h_2(0.1)=0.531$ bits. The iteration reaches both to within $10^{-6}$.
